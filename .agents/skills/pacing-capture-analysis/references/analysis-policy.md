# Pacing analysis policy

## Preserve continuity

Segment before filtering. Keep original row sequences and define adjacency from the unfiltered segment. A filtered `diff()` must not bridge missing, invalid, overloaded, zero-valued, or regime-transition rows unless continuity is independently proven. In pacing rows, a `sequence` gap is a CSV ring drop and a `chain_break` ends display adjacency.

Split on relevant native swapchain, epoch, target, FG divisor, API/path, output kind, predictor/passthrough/overload state, Present mode, loss window, PC Latency status, or timestamp discontinuity. On Phase 2 captures also split on pipeline changes (`plan_rung`), display source (ETW or DXGI) and injected stalls; a grid epoch marks a regime or late-frame restart, not necessarily a segment boundary. On Phase 4 captures also split on `source_placement` and pre-Sleep engagement (`source_flags` bit 2).

Adaptive cap: on Phase 3 captures every governor step started a grid epoch and restarted the cadence, so steps were segment boundaries. The Phase 4 tracker changes the interval continuously on VRR as silent grid rebases (no epoch, no restart): segment on governor phase transitions (`governor_step` events and their cause) instead of on each `governor_step` value, and treat a rising or falling interval as its own stratum. Only snapped fixed-refresh steps (whole refreshes) still start `governor_step` grid epochs. Since the evidence-gated controller of 2026-10-04 the interval rises only after evidence that frames are late (two cadence restarts within 0.5 s, 6 of the newest 24 frames ready after their target, or more than 10% of a window of at least 1 s restarting), sized by demand, and falls by `release` (to the demand after 0.5 s of headroom) or by `probe` (below it after 1 s without evidence; `probe_failed` means evidence returned after the probe). It holds after a rise for 1.5 s per overload episode of the last 20 s (up to 8 s): treat that hold, the calm time before a probe and a probe itself as separate strata, and check each rise against late frames or restarts just before it. The 2026-10-03 governor (2 s windows, a fall at most once per clean window, doubling after a failed fall) and earlier Phase 4 builds are different controllers, so do not pool captures from before and after those revisions as one controller.

Hold feasibility: since the same revision, an Auto-output capture can contain stretches where the hold stepped aside (`rung_feasibility` value 1, `pipeline_change` reason `infeasible`, rows with `plan_rung` 0). Current builds (from 2026-10-07) have one cause: overload (`overload`, every Auto hold; `retry` ends it), besides the not-held blocks of the developer A/B interleave. Builds of 2026-10-05/06 also had the saturation fallback of the GPU queue wait (`saturated`: more than 32 of the newest 128 waited resumes late, more than 128 of 512 in its first build `d98efa17`; `load_dropped` (5 points below the load at the fallback and, from 2026-10-06, at most 78%) or `recheck` (60 s) ended it; `fg_input_wait` and marker holds were unaffected), and interim 2026-10-04/05 builds a 2 s slow-resume pause (`slow_resumes`) and a display-jitter scorecard (`score_not_held`, `score_held`, `probe_not_held`, `probe_held`); do not pool captures of different rules (`d98efa17` 129 of 512, `7b00f9dc` 33 of 128 without the 78% return limit, `c1696c4a` with it, and the collision-aware release). Split on those events and report the share of rows and time in each state and cause; do not pool held and unheld stretches in one hold or jitter comparison, and do not read a lower hold share as a better candidate when the hold was demoted. Captures of CPU-bound scenes (`source_late` grid epochs every second, overload flag on most frames) are a different regime: there held stretches had 6-113 long frames per 1,000 against 0-5 not held (54788_1), so do not pool them with GPU-bound captures. Not-held frames keep the GPU queue wait's lead; in 49596_1 (scorecard build) a held -> not-held switch moved the display 3-6 ms because they did not.

Collision-aware release (from 2026-10-07, Auto GPU queue wait): late GPU resumes (`u − o` above 1 ms) and the 5-8 ms stalls come from frame n + 1's native Present call landing within about 2-2.5 ms after frame n's release Signal `o` (the next-Present margin `p_{n+1} − o_n`), not from GPU load as such. The lead is limited to `I + Q10(C) − M`, with `C` the release-to-Present time `p − s` of the newest 256 GPU queue wait frames and `M` the release margin (2.5 ms; learned in 1-4 ms with the log's `Release margin: A -> B ms (...)` lines in builds from 2026-10-07 to 2026-10-20, fixed at 2.5 ms since, with no such lines and no event). Frames that would need more lead reach the gate after the Signal (`gate_pre_wait_qpc` after `gate_signal_before_qpc`, not waited) and are shown when ready. Report:
- Late share of waited resumes per 0.25 ms band of the next-Present margin, with counts, against the earlier captures (54788_3, 51232_1, 45760_1: 47-56% late under 1.5 ms, 20-54% at 1.5-2 ms, 0.8-2.7% at 2-2.5 ms, 0.00-0.27% from 2.5 ms up), and the share of waited resumes below the margin in force.
- The guard's cost: the share of held frames whose GPU reached the gate after the Signal, and how late (`w − o`: replay expectation about 3% of frames in 54788_3-like scenes and about 26% in the heavier 48860_1 scene at 2.5 ms, 0.3-0.7 ms p50; measured in 10476_1 at 2.5 ms: 10.3% of frames, 74.8% of those at the limit, 0.39 ms p50), beside long frames and adjacent jitter above 2 / 4 ms per 1,000. Check the analyzer's `resume` block first: a `waited_share` far below the earlier captures (54788_3: 86%, 10476_1: 89.7%) means the limit is cutting most leads, as in 48860_1 (1.2% at a margin of 4.0 ms, display jitter p50 438 µs). Reference for a working guard (10476_1): waited resumes with the next Present within 2.25 ms 0.67%, late resumes 0.05% of waited, display jitter p50 39 µs, >2 / >4 ms 13.2 / 2.2 per 1,000, long frames 0.43 per 1,000.
- Plan leads against the limit: guarded frames have `plan_lead_us` at `I + Q10(C) − M`; a lead well above it on a GPU queue wait frame with Output override Auto points at a wiring fault.
- Builds before 2026-10-20 only (they learned the margin; current builds fix it at 2.5 ms and write no step lines): the margin's steps from the log, with the margin bands' late shares before each step. Addon `60f65c2a` (the first build of the change) could jump several bins at once on lateness far above the collision window; from `445b9de5` widening ran only through colliding bands reached upward from the edge, so a jump from 2.5 ms straight to 4 ms in a later capture pointed at a fault. A step down needed minutes of clean evidence, so steps down within a heavy session were suspicious, and a margin that was too wide could not narrow while frames mostly missed the wait (no waited resumes, no evidence).

Release headroom (from 2026-10-08, Auto GPU queue wait with Reflex latency reports): the collision reference is the next frame's heavy GPU work, not its Present call. A late resume is the work after the GPU wait queued behind one of the next frame's long command buffers on the same engine (GPU trace of session 31224; the GPU does not preempt them), and that work starts at about `r − A` (`ready_gpu_qpc` minus `reflex_gpu_active_render_us`). While current samples exist the lead is limited to `I + Q10(D) − H`, `D = r − A − s` over the newest 256 GPU queue wait frames and `H` the release headroom (learned in 0.75-3 ms with log lines `Release headroom: A -> B ms (...)` until 2026-10-20; fixed at 1.0 ms since, developer key `gpu_wait_headroom_us` 500-3000 µs, logged in the `Pacing pipeline:` line); without a Reflex GPU active time within 64 GPU queue wait releases the margin limit above applies (log `Collision guard: release margin ...`, back with `Collision guard: release headroom ...`). Since 2026-10-20 a limit that relaxes returns to the unguarded lead at the lead slew (100 µs per frame, at most I/64), never in one step, and the queue-order stand-aside needs a current heavy-work window (after a lifecycle reset it waits for 32 frames; the pairs are kept). Late resumes are measured (OSD, `resume` block) and nothing acts on them. The Adaptive cap hold room is `Q_fit(X) + 0.25 ms − (Q10(D) − H)` (or `− (Q10(C) − M)`) with the guard's current distances. Report, besides the margin items:
- Late share of waited resumes per band of the headroom `h_n = (r_{n+1} − A_{n+1}) − o_n` (rows n and n + 1 consecutive GPU queue wait frames): before the change, at least 84% late below 0.1 ms, about 11% at 0.3-0.4 ms, at most 1.3% from 0.5 ms and at most 0.5% from 0.75 ms over five probed sessions; minutes 6-8 of 36720_1 still had 4.1% at 0.5-0.75 ms (predicted headroom), the case the former learner had to widen for. Current builds fix H, so compare these bands with the late share at the fixed H instead of waiting for a step.
- Plan leads at `I + Q10(D) − H` on guarded frames, and which guard was in force (the log's `Collision guard:` lines; the OSD line names it). A capture without Reflex reports must stay on the margin.
- The cost against the margin: frames reaching the gate after the Signal (replay: 14.5% / 12.8% at 1.0 ms against 13.1% / 12.9% at the 2.5 ms margin in nine pre-guard / five guarded sessions; 25.3% against 47.6% in 31224_1) and late resumes (523 / 103 against 1,659 / 619). Re-run `tools/hold_sim/headroom_replay.py --captures-dir <dir> --capture <id>` to compare a new session's measured values with the replay.

Hold room (from 2026-10-08, Adaptive cap on, Auto GPU queue wait): the Adaptive cap keeps the interval at least at the Fit percentile of `R = X − (limit − I)` over the newest 256 guard-judged GPU queue wait frames not late against their unguarded lead, the interval at which they reach the gate before their guarded release (`I + Q10(D) − H`, or the margin's limit). The room for a wait is about `I − A` (`(r_{n+1} − A_{n+1}) − w_n`: p50 842 µs at GPU busy 0.89 in 25536_1, 1,506 µs at 0.81 in 31224_1), so without it a GPU-heavy scene holds few frames however the guard is set. `governor_step` reason 8 (`room`) marks where it starts and stops setting the interval; the log adds `hold room N us`; rows keep bit 64. Report:
- Stretch time and size by cause: `room` stretches against demand/misses stretches, frame-rate cost (mean and p90 stretch) and the hold's yield (waited share, late arrivals) inside room stretches against the same GPU busy bands without them (25536_1 before the change: 14.2% of frames waited overall, 6.3% at 0.86-0.90 busy).
- Display jitter p50 and >1 / 2 / 4 ms in room stretches against the replay (`tools/hold_sim/headroom_replay.py --captures-dir <dir> --hold-room --capture <id>`; 25536_1 predicted +6.9% interval, jitter p50 299 → 211 µs, >1 ms 70.2 → 22.8 per 1,000).
- Whether the room tracks the interval it buys: A (`reflex_gpu_active_render_us`) and D at the room's interval against the same scene at the cap. The design assumes they do not move (release falls of 20 sessions: −0.01 and +0.06 µs per µs); a room that keeps rising while the scene's load is steady points at clocks dropping with load (A grows) or a render-submit stage stretching D's spread.
- Late resumes inside room stretches (the replay expects no change, 626 → 630 over 15 sessions).

Gated holds (lockstep engines, addon `7fe91ccd` and later, 2026-10-08): some engines finish frame n's render submission and Present only after ReLimiter releases frame n + 1 (CONTROL Resonant 29380_3: Present(n) − `source_release_qpc`(n + 1) p50 +3.1 ms where the next wait exceeded 2 ms, −3.3 ms with the Adaptive cap off; render submission(n) correlated 0.68 with the next wait). Frame n's gated hold is the overlap of `[source_release_qpc(n), Present-side time(n)]` with the waits (source wait, pre-Sleep wait) of later frames released no later than that time; current builds judge late outputs, the Adaptive cap's slack and the Reflex demand (simulation, render submission; stable clock map only) without it, but not X or the lead. Rebuild it from `source_wait_start_qpc` → `source_release_qpc` and `pre_sleep_start_qpc` → `pre_sleep_end_qpc` (138-column files); 136-column files have neither start, so use `source_marker_qpc` for the source wait and leave pre-Sleep waits out, and say so. Take the Present-side time from `reflex_local_present_start_qpc` (ReLimiter's PRESENT_START hook) on source-only and marker hold, not from `present_marker_qpc`, which is empty when the Present call is not attributed on the marker's thread (`join_rule` 2, every planned row of 29380 with DLSS-G). Report:
- Gated share and gated hold p50 per Adaptive cap band (29380_3 replay: 36.8%, 12.5 ms; 29380_1: 1.3%, 15 µs; Rogue Core 42520_1 / 10476_1: none). A serial engine (s_{n+1} after Present(n)) must stay at zero.
- Reflex demand (max of render submission and GPU active) p50 per interval band with and without the gated hold: corrected it should stay flat across bands (29380_3: 14.4-14.8 ms from 14 to 21 ms; raw render submission 4.9 → 15.0 ms). A demand that still grows with the interval points at a stage the correction does not cover.
- The Adaptive cap's stretch against GPU active / I_0 (29380: about 14.5 / 12.7 ms, so about +14% remains real overload, not the former +60%).
- With frame generation, the GPU demand includes `reflex_ai_frame_time_us` (frame generation runs on the same GPU within the real frame's interval) in builds after `bd02178`; before that the demand was GPU active time alone, and every `release` fall landed below the GPU's per-frame work (38440_5: active 13.8 + AI 1.42 ms p50; 17 of 20 OutputLate epochs and 32 of 84 jitter > 4 ms events within 1 s after the 8 falls). Check falls against active + AI time, and judge the late frames and display jitter in the second after each `release` and `probe` against the rest of the capture.
- Probes (builds after `6cde395`): none starts while GPU execution time (plus AI frame time) is the largest stage in most reports of the newest second, and a probe waits a calm second after any fall. Before that, failed probes on GPU-bound frames were most of the Adaptive cap's display jitter outside overload: in 32724_3 (FG off, 120, `gpu_queue_wait`) the windows from each failed probe to 0.5 s after it held 7% of frames but jitter > 2 / 4 ms of 141 / 38.5 per 1,000 against 8.4 / 1.5 elsewhere; in 38440_5 both probes followed a `release` fall within one frame. In a new capture count `probe` events against `probe_failed` and the jitter in the second after each.
- Release falls (builds after `1079dd9`) go to max(Q90 of the newest 12 reports, Q_fit of the newest second) + m, the need a rise uses; earlier builds fell to Q_fit + m. Before the change about half the falls were reversed by a demand rise within 2 s (Rogue Core 40528_3: 37 of 81; CONTROL 42072_1: 3 of 7) and carried the jitter of falls (40528_3: jitter > 2 / 4 ms 56.9 / 9.0 per 1,000 in the second after a reversed fall against 12.4 / 0.9 after one that held; background 23.5 / 3.5). Count falls reversed within 2 s and the jitter after them.
  - Rogue Core 7980_1 (`c9b9b72`, 874 s): falls reversed within 2 s 25% (40528_3: 48%); jitter > 4 ms in the second after a fall 3.58 against 5.21 per 1,000 elsewhere (40528_3: 4.45 against 3.47).
- Release headroom and margin steps (builds before 2026-10-20, which learned them; `Release headroom:` / `Release margin:` log lines; none in the 20 archived logs with the headroom guard before 7980). Before the build after `c9b9b72` a widening never narrowed: the guard keeps releases about 0.5 ms above the edge, so the bin just below it never reached the 512 weighted resumes the clean rule needs. 7980: H 1.00 → 2.25 ms at 51 s of startup (191 of 1,984 waited resumes late, 23 s before capture), then 0 of 68,696 waited resumes late at any distance and at most 199 weighted in [1.5, 1.75) ms. The hold room grows with H one for one (R = X − Q10(D) + H): 78.9% of frames room-set (40528_3 at H = 1.0: 9.5%), output 111.5 FPS (124.0), waited share 70.8% (87.4%), and frames reaching the wait after their release had display jitter p50 308-803 µs against 36-97 µs held. Later builds narrowed above the start once the bin below the edge held fewer than 4 weighted late resumes. Across about 1.35 million waited resumes in 47 Rogue Core captures with probes and Reflex GPU times, late resumes were 0.00-0.05% per 250 µs distance bin from 1 ms on and 99.2% of all late resumes were closer than 1 ms, so builds from 2026-10-20 fix H at 1.0 ms and M at 2.5 ms and write no step lines: in a new capture read `gpu_wait_headroom_us` from the `Pacing pipeline:` line and judge the waited share, late share and room-set share against it.

Render pipeline (Reflex marker games, CONTROL Resonant 2026-10-09/10): with the Reflex Sleep bypassed (default `reflex_sleep_bypass=true`) nothing but ReLimiter decides when the game's simulation starts, so where it runs against the game's render thread decides both latency and pacing. Rebuild each real frame's markers in local QPC (`reflex_local_present_start_qpc` + (marker µs − `reflex_present_start_us`)); never subtract Reflex microseconds from QPC values directly. `scripts/render_pipeline.py <captures...>` does this and reports, per held / not-held segment: the stages (simulation, SIMULATION_END → RENDERSUBMIT_START queue, submission RENDERSUBMIT_START → RENDERSUBMIT_END, Present call, GPU render, release → display, display − T, release − S), the reconstructed render handoff limit (share released at or after RENDERSUBMIT_START(n − 1), the offset d), where each frame's simulation starts against frame n − 1's render-thread work, with that submission's time and how often the simulation outlasted the render thread, segment display pacing and grid epochs per minute. Report:
- Segments: held (`plan_rung` > 0) and not held separately; an overload pause (`rung_feasibility` 1 → 0) is a not-held stretch inside an Auto capture, and the first seconds after each transition (`--settle-seconds`, default 2) belong to neither. 38644_1 had one 4.1 s pause in an Auto capture; its held frames regressed on their own (long frames 11.2 per 1,000 outside the pause), so report the pause apart instead of attributing the regression to it.
- Simulation start position: in CONTROL the submission is shortest when the next simulation starts after it, in the Present wait (35924_5 / 13844_5: 6.76 / 6.83 ms against 7.43–7.96 ms when it starts before or during it), but simulations started there outlast the render thread on 74–79% of frames. Forcing simulations into the submission slowed it to 10.7 ms p50 (38644_1, first half). A shift in these shares between builds is the first thing to check when held pacing changes.
- The queue in front of the render thread (SIMULATION_END → RENDERSUBMIT_START) is not waste on held frames: it absorbs simulation variance the output hold (0.7 ms p50 at 130 FG 2×) cannot. Judge a latency change against long frames and OutputLate epochs per minute in the same table.
- Render handoff limit (frames not held, from 2026-10-10): `wake_target_qpc` past S on most frames and one SourceLate epoch per limited frame (35924_1 at 157: about 1,000 per minute) are the limit taking its earliest release as the arrival, not overload by themselves; judge the pause by `rung_feasibility` and the display metrics. The log's `Render handoff limit:` lines mark the first limited frame, stalls (3 render-thread frame times without any handoff, at least 3 intervals) and blocks; a WARN `off` line in a game that runs ahead of its render thread is a defect (telemetry semantics, log lines). Only build `766a4539` (38644_1) also limited held frames.
- Back-pressure (from 2026-10-11, the render handoff limit's GPU floor: a learned term through `a4a139b`, measured after it): in a segment `render_pipeline.py` classifies each frame from its and the previous frame's Reflex report: held back (the GPU waited at most 1 ms and the driver received the frame's first work only in the second half of the render thread's window or later, while the render thread came straight from the previous frame or the game had already shown 32 pairs with the first work in the first half of the window) or released late (the GPU waited more than 1 ms while the render thread waited for the simulation). The measured floor releases a frame that is not held so that `RS_START − GPU end(n-1)` is no lower than −(P/2 + F) (P the median GPU period of consecutive frames the GPU did not wait for, F the median first submission − RS_START of streaming frames); the script prints that position from the segment's own frames beside the measured one, so a not-held segment under the floor should show its `RS_START − GPU end(n-1)` low end near it and few held-back run starts per second. The learned term (through `a4a139b`) rose one step per held-back frame, fell one per late frame and one per 1,024 clean frames (builds `a0549e8` and earlier aimed for about 5% held back). In Gears of War: E-Day 120 FG the render thread was held back on 70–96% of frames that started more than 11 ms before the previous GPU end and on 1–5% of those that started later. Held-back frames come in runs (P(held back | previous held back) 0.88 in 44076_1); only the run's first frame stutters (GPU frame stretched in 69%, a long displayed frame in 59%; 0–3% for the rest), so count run starts per second beside the held-back share. Not held, run starts were 1–3% of frames at −12 to −10 ms and none from −9.5 to −7.5 ms; held frames reached −4 ms without being held back. References: Gears 120 FG paused 46012_1 (render-thread offset only, reports 250 ms late) held back 77.3%, late 4.8%, RS_START − GPU end(n-1) p50 −13.7 ms, simulation start → GPU start 19.9 ms; 44076_1 (`a0549e8`, 5% target) 5.0%, 0.0%, −10.5 ms, 16.8 ms, 1.02 run starts/s, −(P/2 + F) −9.38 ms, long 11.5 and jitter > 4 ms 70.7 per 1,000; 44076_3 (held, Adaptive) 37.4%, 0.66 run starts/s, p50 −16.1 ms; 45072_1 (term absent) 87.4%, 0.0%, −18.1 ms, 29.8 ms; driver's Sleep 46012_5 / 45072_3 0.0% / 1.5%, −9.4 ms, 14.5–15.2 ms; CONTROL 157 FG paused 38440_1 0.0%, 0.4%, −3.9 ms, 15.5 ms (its driver Sleep capture 42620_1: 22% released late); Rogue Core paused 632_7 0.0%, 0.0%, −4.4 ms. Gears' render thread waits for the GPU inside its window, so a longer submission there is a wait, not CPU contention. Compare these shares, the position, simulation start → GPU start and real-frame PC Latency beside long frames and jitter in the same scene. Reflex reports reached ReLimiter late when one report per poll left a backlog (Gears: `reflex_report_qpc` − GPU end about 250 ms against about 15 ms in Rogue Core); every unseen report is taken per poll since `a0549e8` (Gears then about 35 ms, two frames), so check that lag before judging any report-driven loop.
- Latency: real-frame PC Latency (rows with `real_frame` 1) beside all-row PC Latency; with FG the all-row value includes generated frames and moved twice as much as the real-frame value in 38644_1 (−2.6 against −1.3 ms). Latency conclusions need Reflex active (FG forces it on).

Replays and simulators: a replay that keeps the game's render-thread timing as captured can reject a release rule, never approve one. In 38644_1 a first-order replay of RENDERSUBMIT_START(n − 1) + 2.25 ms predicted 1.1% of frames reaching the render thread later; live, the submission slowed while the simulation overlapped it, long frames rose 1.8 → 11.2 per 1,000 and OutputLate epochs 11.7 → 70.5 per minute. Use `render_pipeline.py --replay-floor ANCHOR[+-MS]`, which carries render-thread delays into later frames until the Present call's back-pressure absorbs them (a floor at PRESENT_START(n − 1) on 35924_5: 4.9% of frames leave Present later than I/4, p99 23.7 ms), and state that CPU contention between the simulation and the submission is not modeled. Any rule a replay does not reject still needs a live A/B in the same scene.

Collision lateness clusters in heavy stretches (54788_3: 0.5-5% late at 1.75-2 ms in the first two minutes, 45-97% in its heaviest 30 s stretches), so compare bands within the same stretches, not pooled across calm and heavy play.

A/B interleave (developer key `gpu_wait_interleave_s`, since 2026-10-05): the Auto GPU queue wait alternates held and not-held blocks of 0.75-1.25 x the key (`rung_feasibility` causes `ab_held` / `ab_not_held`, value 0 / 1); the overload guard and, from 2026-10-07, the collision-aware release apply in held blocks (the wait cap too in builds up to 2026-10-20; the saturation fallback was off in 2026-10-05/06 builds, the scorecard and slow-resume pause in interim builds). This is the only capture type that compares the two outputs without selection bias: captures of rules that choose the output from what they measured compare held and not-held frames at different moments. Drop the switch frame and the next five of every block (switch cost, report it separately), compare held and not-held blocks per 5% GPU load band (`reflex_gpu_active_render_us / interval_us`, 30-frame average) and as adjacent block pairs (paired differences, so slow scene changes cancel), and report stutter rates (adjacent jitter above 2 / 4 ms and long frames per 1,000) beside mean jitter and PC latency. Evaluate candidate hold rules offline by choosing, per block, the output a rule would have picked from earlier blocks only; a rule that reacts faster than a block cannot be judged per block, so check it on the frames inside held blocks (for example the held frames in the 2 s after a trigger against not-held frames at the same loads, as for 45760_1). Split held frames by their next-Present margin: in 45760_1 held frames without a collision had 11.8 / 0.2 jitter events above 2 / 4 ms and 0.06 long frames per 1,000, collided held frames 62.8 / 17.4 / 1.90, not-held frames 102.2 / 17.8 / 1.96.

GPU wait cap (builds from 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-20 only; removed on 2026-10-20, so current builds write no `gpu_wait_cap` events): from 2026-10-04 the Auto GPU queue wait was capped by a ladder (`gpu_wait_cap` events, level 0..3; a higher level is a lower cap) instead of being switched off for late resumes; nothing stopped it for late resumes from 2026-10-07 (the saturation fallback, `rung_feasibility` cause `saturated`, did in 2026-10-05/06 builds, and the 2 s `slow_resumes` pause in interim builds; the ladder kept its level across them). From 2026-10-09 level 255 was the open top above level 0 (no cap; Auto games whose next frame's GPU work runs behind the wait, causes `opened`/`closed`): treat it as its own level, with the longest lead. For captures from those builds, split on those events and report the share of rows and time at each level; do not pool rows at different levels in one GPU-queue-wait residence, resume or jitter comparison, and do not compare a capture from before the ladder or from after its removal with one from those builds as if only the cap differed. Cap levels follow scene difficulty, so do not read a level's worse jitter as caused by that level without a same-scene comparison. The removal's reason: at a fixed distance to the next frame's heavy GPU work, short holds were the late ones, so a cap on the hold length pushed the wrong way; the collision-aware release is the one mechanism against late resumes.

## Robust summaries

Prefer interval-domain statistics:

- Count and observed duration
- Median and MAD or IQR
- p95 and p99 with the quantile convention stated
- Absolute and target-relative error bands
- Events per second and event run lengths
- Lag-1 and sign alternation only on contiguous observations
- Context windows around worst events and state transitions

Keep interval statistics primary. $10^6 / \operatorname{mean}(interval)$ is not the same as mean instantaneous FPS.

Runtime snapshots, `pcl_i2fs_us` and the adaptation offset in `interval_us` are windowed or EMA outputs. Averaging their rows is not equivalent to recomputing quality from independent samples.

## Suggested event definitions

Choose thresholds before comparing variants and report sensitivity to nearby thresholds:

- Mild target miss: display interval outside ±5% of its row's T_out
- Severe target miss: display interval outside ±15% of its row's T_out
- Long frame: display interval above 1.2× or 1.5× T_out (the runtime `long_frame` flag uses 1.5×)
- Adjacent jitter event: `adjacent_jitter_us` above 3 ms
- Hold active: `cpu_hold_us` or `gpu_residence_us` above 10 µs; also report a stricter `> 100 µs` sensitivity view

These are analysis conventions, not product guarantees.

## Question-specific exclusions

- Nominal pacing may summarize warm non-overload strata, but must also disclose overload prevalence and excluded runs.
- Stutter and overload analyses must retain overload rows.
- Stall-recovery analyses keep the injected-stall rows and their following intervals; exclude them from nominal pacing instead (`source_flags` bit 128 marks the stalled release).
- Hold comparisons are observational: holds are keyed to their own Present, but a frame without a hold is not proven eligible for one.
- Preserve row identity. Never filter two columns separately and then zip them.
- Do not sum GPU-active, AI, Reflex stage, CPU hold, GPU residence, or PC Latency components unless source semantics prove they are non-overlapping components.
- DMFG passthrough rows do not support paced-target, hold, or headroom conclusions.

## Comparisons and uncertainty

- Use runs as independent units; rows within a run are autocorrelated.
- Prefer paired/alternating run order in the same fixed scenario.
- For within-run intervals, use stable time blocks or a moving-block bootstrap rather than IID row resampling.
- Report each run and the aggregate. A pooled row distribution can hide run-to-run variance.
- p99 claims need far more stable samples than median claims; target at least 10,000 stable frames per cell and preferably 30,000 or more.
- Flag metadata differences before calculating “improvement.” Scene, thermals, clocks, driver, target, refresh, API/path, and FG state can dominate scheduler changes.

## Comparisons to reject

- Source release or API Present intervals presented as displayed output
- Real-frame metrics presented as FG output cadence
- Raw QPC subtraction against FrameView seconds without proven synchronization
- Reflex driver-domain microseconds subtracted from QPC-derived values, or across rows
- Pacing/FrameView row-number joins
- Legacy-family fields mapped onto pacing columns, or unknown historical aliases treated as current semantics
- Exact equality grouping of fractional FG divisor or adaptive target values
- Empty cells treated as zero, or zero treated uniformly as either valid data or missing data across all fields
- A single baseline/candidate capture treated as causal proof
- Simulator or reconstructed shadow output treated as closed-loop runtime evidence
